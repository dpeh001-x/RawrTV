## EP.02 — "JURASSIC PARK LIED TO YOU" (Velociraptor)

**Hook:** "The scariest dino in the movie? In real life it was a turkey. With knives."
**Truth:** Real Velociraptor was ~2m long — but most of that was tail. About 15kg, knee-high, roughly turkey- or medium-dog-sized. And **feathered**: quill knobs on a fossil forearm proved it had wing-feathers on its arms (it just couldn't fly). The movie "raptors" were oversized and scaly — both wrong.
**Punchline:** "Hollywood's apex predator was an angry feathered turkey. Respect the drip though."

**Clips (4):**
1. ⭐ Feathered turkey-sized Velociraptor standing alert in a Gobi desert dune landscape, wing-like feathers on its arms ruffling in wind, warm low sun, telephoto handheld wildlife shot, photorealistic, no text.
2. Close-up of a feathered dromaeosaur forearm, rows of long quill feathers anchored along the bone, sharp feather-barb detail, soft rim light, shallow depth of field, photorealistic, no text.
3. Extreme close-up of a raised sickle claw on the second toe, dust and scales, dramatic side light, photorealistic macro, no text.
4. Feathered Velociraptor sprinting low across sandy ground, motion blur, kicked-up dust, overcast light, handheld chase-cam, photorealistic, no text.

**Pin:** Size ~15kg / turkey-sized: NHM, AMNH. Feathers = quill knobs, Turner et al. 2007, *Science*. We don't make facts up. 🦖

## EP.03 — "NERFED FOR 80 YEARS STRAIGHT" (Spinosaurus)

**Hook:** "This dino got patched more times than any video game character alive."
**Truth:** The first and only good Spinosaurus skeleton was **blown up in an Allied bombing of a Munich museum in 1944** — so for decades nobody really knew what it looked like. Then it kept getting redesigned: 2014 gave it short legs and a semi-aquatic body, 2020 gave it a paddle-shaped tail — making it the first dinosaur we think actually *swam*. Bigger than T-Rex, too.
**Punchline:** "Bro's been getting patch notes since 1944 and he's STILL broken. Nerf incoming."

**Clips (4):**
1. ⭐ Massive Spinosaurus wading through a wide prehistoric river, tall sail backlit by golden sun, water rippling around its legs, slow telephoto push-in, photorealistic, cinematic, no text.
2. Underwater side view of Spinosaurus swimming with a paddle-like tail sweeping, shafts of light through murky water, bubbles, photorealistic, no text.
3. Close-up of the tall spinal sail, skin stretched over long spines, water droplets running off, dramatic overcast light, photorealistic detail, no text.
4. Spinosaurus snapping its long crocodile-like snout at a fish in shallow water, splash and spray, dynamic handheld, photorealistic, no text.

**Pin:** Holotype destroyed in WWII bombing of Munich, 1944. Semi-aquatic redesign: Ibrahim et al. 2014, *Science*; paddle tail: Ibrahim et al. 2020, *Nature*. 🦖

## EP.04 — "NOT EVEN A DINOSAUR" (Mosasaurus)

**Hook:** "That giant sea monster in Jurassic World? Technically not a dinosaur. At all."
**Truth:** Mosasaurus was a **marine reptile** — a squamate, more closely related to modern **monitor lizards and snakes** than to any dinosaur. The real one was ~12–17m (still enormous, but the movie blew it up several times bigger). It ruled the oceans right up to the end of the Cretaceous.
**Punchline:** "So it's basically a 50-foot swimming Komodo dragon. Which is honestly worse."

**Clips (4):**
1. ⭐ Enormous Mosasaurus breaching the surface of a Cretaceous ocean, water sheeting off its body, dramatic backlit spray, slow-motion, photorealistic, cinematic wildlife, no text.
2. Underwater side view of a Mosasaurus gliding with four paddle limbs and a finned tail, deep blue light, silhouetted fish scattering, photorealistic, no text.
3. Close-up of a Mosasaurus head and jaws opening, conical teeth, wet scaly skin, shafts of underwater light, photorealistic detail, no text.
4. Low-angle shot looking up as the Mosasaurus passes overhead against bright sun-dappled surface, god rays, photorealistic, ominous, no text.

**Pin:** Mosasaurus = marine squamate reptile (related to monitor lizards/snakes), not a dinosaur. Real length ~12–17m. Consensus paleontology. 🦖

## EP.05 — "A MYSTERY FOR 50 YEARS" (Deinocheirus)

**Hook:** "In 1965 they dug up these giant arms — and had NO idea what the rest looked like for 50 years."
**Truth:** A pair of 2.4m arms turned up in Mongolia in 1965, named "terrible hand." Everyone assumed a super-predator bigger than T-Rex. Then in 2014 full skeletons finally revealed it: an ~11m, humpbacked, **duck-billed, pot-bellied omnivore** — basically a giant ostrich-dino. Bonus: its skull was looted by poachers and had to be repatriated to Mongolia.
**Punchline:** "Everyone expected a monster. We got a 6-ton feathered goose with a beer belly. And it's REAL."

**Clips (4):**
1. Two giant clawed dinosaur arms half-buried in Gobi desert sand at dawn, isolated and mysterious, long shadows, telephoto, photorealistic, eerie, no text.
2. ⭐ Full reveal of Deinocheirus — a huge humpbacked, duck-billed ornithomimosaur wading in a shallow wetland, sail-hump on its back, feathers, morning mist, slow telephoto, photorealistic, no text.
3. Close-up of a broad duck-like bill dipping into water to feed on plants, water dripping, soft light, photorealistic, no text.
4. Wide silhouette of Deinocheirus at dusk showing its humped back and long arms against an orange sky, reeds in foreground, photorealistic, moody, no text.

**Pin:** Arms found 1965, named 1970. Full body: Lee et al. 2014, *Nature*. Looted skull later repatriated to Mongolia. 🦖
